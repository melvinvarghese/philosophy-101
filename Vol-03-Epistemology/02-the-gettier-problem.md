# The Gettier Problem: Is "Justified True Belief" Enough?

For a long time, philosophers defined Knowledge as **JTB**:
1. You believe it.
2. It is actually true.
3. You have a good reason (justification) for believing it.

**Enter Edmund Gettier (1963).**
He broke this definition.

**The Scenario:**
You look at a clock. It says 2:00. You believe it's 2:00. It *is* actually 2:00.
BUT, the clock actually broke yesterday at exactly 2:00. You just got lucky.
* Did you *know* it was 2:00?
* No! It was luck. But you had Justified True Belief.

This shows that "Knowledge" is harder to define than we thought.

### Reflection
*How many things do you "know" that might just be lucky guesses?*
